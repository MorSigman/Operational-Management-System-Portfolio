# Overview

## The problem

The business this system was built for runs a physical manufacturing
process — producing insulated modular structures — through a chain of
departments: sales/quoting, engineering, production, warehousing, field
installation, and finance. Before this system existed, that chain was
coordinated through spreadsheets, messaging apps, and paper, which meant
status information was scattered, out of date, and error-prone — especially
across departments that don't share a desk (shop floor vs. office vs. field
crews).

## The goal

Replace that fragmented process with a single system that:

- reflects the *real* physical status of every project, room, and
  component at all times
- is usable directly on the shop floor and in the field, not just in an
  office
- removes manual re-entry of the same information across tools
- automates calculations that were previously done by hand (pricing, bill
  of materials, payment milestones)
- works reliably with multiple people editing simultaneously

## Who uses it

- **Management / office staff** — full visibility across all active
  projects, pricing, and finances
- **Production floor** — real-time component status and station assignment
- **Field installation teams** — daily/weekly scheduling and installation
  status updates
- **Warehouse** — inventory and purchase order tracking
- **HR** — staffing, attendance, and scheduling

## My role

I designed and built the entire system independently — data model, business
logic, UI/UX, and the real-time sync architecture — iterating directly with
the people using it daily on the shop floor and in the office.
